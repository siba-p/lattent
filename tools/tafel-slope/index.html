Tafel slope and exchange current density

Paste current density against overpotential from the linear Tafel region of your polarisation curve. Lattent fits η = a + b·log₁₀|j| and reports the Tafel slope in mV per decade and the exchange current density, each with a confidence interval.

Free, no sign-up. The analysis runs in your browser; your data is not uploaded.

What you get

  • Tafel slope b in mV/dec with a 95% confidence interval
  • Exchange current density j₀ from the intercept
  • Residuals that show where the Tafel region ends
  • A log-scale figure ready for the paper

η = a + b log10|j|,   j0 = 10−a/b

Questions

Which part of the curve should I paste?

Only the straight part on a log scale: beyond the first few tens of millivolts, where the back reaction still matters, and before mass transport bends the curve. Correct for iR drop first.

What do common HER Tafel slopes mean?

At 25 °C, about 120, 40 and 30 mV/dec are classically linked to the Volmer, Heyrovsky and Tafel steps being rate-determining. Real catalysts often fall in between.

Cathodic currents are negative. Is that a problem?

Use magnitudes: paste |j| and |η|. The log needs positive current densities.
