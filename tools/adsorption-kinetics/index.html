Adsorption kinetics: pseudo-first and pseudo-second order

Paste uptake against time (qₜ in mg/g, t in minutes). Lattent fits the Lagergren pseudo-first-order and the Ho–McKay pseudo-second-order models directly, without linearising, so you get honest uncertainties and a fair comparison.

Free, no sign-up. The analysis runs in your browser; your data is not uploaded.

What you get

  • Equilibrium uptake qₑ and rate constants k₁ and k₂ with 95% confidence intervals
  • The initial adsorption rate h = k₂qₑ²
  • AICc for each model, so you can say which one the data support
  • Residual plots that show systematic misfit

qt = qe(1 − e−k₁t),   qt = k₂qe²t / (1 + k₂qet)

Questions

Why not use the linear t/qₜ plot?

The linearised pseudo-second-order plot gives a high R² for almost any saturating data, because t appears on both axes. Fitting the equation itself avoids that bias and gives uncertainties you can report.

Which model fits better?

Compare AICc, not R². The model with the lower AICc is better supported; a difference of more than about 2 is meaningful. A better pseudo-second-order fit does not by itself prove chemisorption.

What are the units of k?

k₁ is in 1/min when time is in minutes. k₂ is in g/(mg·min) when qₜ is in mg/g.
