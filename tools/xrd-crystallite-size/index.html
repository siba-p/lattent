XRD crystallite size

Paste a diffractogram (2θ and intensity). Lattent removes the baseline, fits every peak with pseudo-Voigt profiles and reports d-spacings, Scherrer crystallite size and Williamson–Hall size and microstrain.

Free, no sign-up. The analysis runs in your browser; your data is not uploaded.

What you get

  • Peak positions, heights, FWHM and areas with uncertainties
  • d-spacing from Bragg's law for any common anode
  • Scherrer size with instrumental broadening removed
  • Williamson–Hall size and microstrain from all peaks

Questions

Which wavelength is used?

Cu Kα (1.5406 Å) by default; Co, Mo and Fe Kα can be chosen in Studio.

Why correct for instrument broadening?

Every diffractometer broadens peaks. Subtract a standard's FWHM (for example LaB₆ or Si) in quadrature, or the size will be underestimated.

Scherrer or Williamson–Hall?

Scherrer assumes all broadening comes from size, so it gives a lower bound. Williamson–Hall separates size from strain when you have at least three peaks.
