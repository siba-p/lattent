Validation

Lattent's analysis engine is a small, readable JavaScript library. The same reference datasets are fitted by Lattent and by SciPy, the standard scientific Python library, and the results must agree before any release. The test suites (tests/run-engines.js with tests/check_scipy.py for the calculators, and tests/run-studio.js with tests/check_studio.py for Studio) simulates data with known parameters and noise, fits it in both, and compares estimates, standard errors, derived quantities and p-values.

Lattent's specialist models

The co-scientist's built-in suggestions and the shape-guided auto-fit use two small neural networks trained by Lattent, not by a third party. Both run entirely in your browser.

  • Shape model. It recognises 13 families of behaviour from XY data. Training used 67,600 simulated datasets with realistic ranges, spacings, replicates, outliers and noise. On 7,800 held-out datasets it is 98.4% correct first time and 99.8% within its top two. It also classifies all 25 realistic lab examples in the test suite correctly, such as a five-point Arrhenius set, XPS with a step background and a dilution-series calibration line.
  • Scatter model. It judges whether measurement error grows with the signal, so that 1/Y² weighting is advised. Held-out accuracy is 72% (AUC 0.82). Without replicates this is a hard problem, so Lattent acts on it only when the model is at least 80% confident and the data span a wide range.

Discovery and generative models

Discover combines a live database, a powder-diffraction simulator and three neural networks that Lattent trained itself on Materials Project data (via Matbench, CC BY 4.0). The networks run in your browser, and the browser code reproduces PyTorch to within 2×10⁻⁶ (float32 property networks) and 1.2×10⁻³ (float16 generator).

  • Database. Searches query the Alexandria database (Schmidt, Marques and co-workers; 5.8 million PBE and 0.4 million PBEsol structures, CC BY 4.0) directly from your browser through the OPTIMADE API. Formation energies, hull distances and band gaps are Alexandria’s DFT values.
  • XRD simulation. Reflections are generated in the conventional cell after the stored cell is symmetrised to its reported crystal system. Intensities use |F|² with approximate atomic scattering factors, the Lorentz–polarisation factor and an isotropic Debye–Waller factor (B = 0.5 Å²), so positions are exact for the DFT cell and intensities are a guide.
  • Phase matching. For each candidate a uniform lattice scale between −4% and +4% is refined, because DFT lattices differ from experiment by 1–2%. The score is √(reference intensity covered × measured intensity explained).
  • Generator. Masked discrete diffusion over five (element, count) slots with confidence-ordered unmasking in ten steps and classifier-free guidance (w = 1.5) on band gap and number of elements; the chemical system is enforced exactly. Candidates must be charge-balanced with common oxidation states and have a negative predicted formation energy.
  • Limits. Generation is at the level of composition, not crystal structure, and a negative formation energy does not establish stability against competing phases. PBE band gaps are typically 30–50% below experiment. Treat every candidate as a lead for a calculation or a synthesis.

Run the checks yourself: node tests/gen/check.js tests/gen/props_parity.json and node tests/gen/check_xtal.js (the second queries Alexandria; add --offline to skip it). Retrain with tests/gen/train_props.py and tests/gen/train_mdiff.py.

What is computed and how

  • Fitting: Levenberg–Marquardt nonlinear least squares with central-difference Jacobians and several starting points. Standard errors come from s²(JᵀWJ)⁻¹, as in SciPy and GraphPad Prism.
  • Confidence intervals: t-distributed with n − k degrees of freedom. IC50 and EC50 intervals are computed on log concentration; derived quantities (ECx, interpolated concentrations, kcat) use the delta method.
  • Model comparison: extra-sum-of-squares F test for nested dose–response models; corrected Akaike criterion and Akaike weights for kinetic models.
  • qPCR: efficiency-corrected relative quantities normalised to the geometric mean of the reference genes (Hellemans et al., Genome Biology 2007), statistics on log₂ scale, Holm correction.

Limits

Lattent is research software. It is not validated for GLP, 21 CFR Part 11 or clinical diagnostic use. Asymptotic confidence intervals assume roughly normal residuals and a well-determined fit; Lattent warns when a parameter is poorly constrained, but judgement is still yours.
