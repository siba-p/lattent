Getting started

How do I start?

Open Studio and choose a worked example close to your field, or create a table and paste your data straight from Excel or an instrument export. Then press Analyze, or open Facet for a suggestion.

Which table type should I use?

XY for curves and spectra (one X column, one or more Y columns; columns with the same title are replicates). Column for comparing groups. Multiple variables for compositions, process settings and properties, one row per sample.

Can I import instrument files?

Yes. Drop them anywhere on Studio, or use Paste data, then Open a file. Supported: CSV, TXT, XY, JCAMP-DX, PANalytical XRDML, Rigaku RAS, Bruker UXD, Thermo SPC, and CIF (which becomes a simulated XRD pattern). Export binary .raw or .brml scans as .xy or .xrdml first. Files are read in the browser and never uploaded.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything runs in your browser and projects are kept there. An account adds cloud storage, your plan on every computer and Lab sharing.

Curve fitting and equations

Which equation should I fit?

Run Auto-fit: it fits every plausible equation for the shape of your data and ranks them by AICc. Equation discovery can also find a formula that is not in the library.

The fit does not converge.

Check that X and Y are in the right columns and units, try Auto-fit, fix a parameter you know, or start from a simpler model. Very few points cannot support many parameters.

Can I type my own equation?

Yes. Choose User-defined equation and write it with x, any parameter names, + − * / ^ and functions such as exp, ln and sqrt.

Statistics

Which test should I use?

Facet suggests one from your data: t test or Mann–Whitney for two groups, ANOVA, Welch’s ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis for more. Lattent checks normality and equal variance for you.

Why not several t tests?

Each extra test raises the chance of a false positive. ANOVA with Tukey or Holm–Šidák post tests corrects for the number of comparisons.

XRD, spectra and band gaps

How is crystallite size calculated?

Peaks are fitted with pseudo-Voigt profiles on a fitted baseline, instrumental broadening is removed, and the Scherrer and Williamson–Hall equations give size and strain. Sizes above about 100 nm are unreliable with any lab diffractometer.

How do I identify the phase?

After a peak analysis with X as 2θ, press Identify phases and list the elements that may be present. Lattent matches your peaks against every computed structure of those elements.

My Tauc fit looks wrong.

Choose the transition type (direct or indirect) and set the linear region by hand around the steepest part of the absorption edge.

Facet and the AI models

What can Facet do without an API key?

Suggest analyses, check your whole project for mistakes, and read data from a picture of a graph. All of this runs offline in your browser.

How do I use the chat?

Open Facet, choose Ask, and add your own Anthropic or OpenAI key; it stays in your browser. You can attach a figure and ask what it shows or what is wrong with it. Analyses it proposes run only after you approve them.

How accurate are the models?

Each model’s accuracy on data it never saw is listed on the AI page and the validation page.

Discover: database, phases, new materials

The database search fails.

Discover queries the public Alexandria database from your browser. Check your connection, or a network that blocks external sites, and try again; very broad searches can be slow.

Are generated materials real discoveries?

They are candidates: compositions predicted to be stable and close to your target band gap, and absent from the database. Confirm them with a calculation or a synthesis.

Troubleshooting

My pasted data lands in one column.

Lattent recognises tab, comma and semicolon separators and decimal commas. If the paste still lands in one column, copy the cells directly from Excel rather than from a PDF, or use Paste or import.

A chart is blank or the page looks broken.

Use a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, reload the page, and turn off extensions that block scripts for this site.

Studio is slow with a large file.

Curve fitting and AutoML work best with up to a few thousand points; for long spectra, crop to the region of interest first.

I lost my project.

Projects live in the browser where you made them. If the browser’s data was cleared and the project was not saved to the cloud or as a file, it cannot be recovered.
